      Three idealized structures

      The beam model uses Euler–Bernoulli bending theory for a straight, uniform beam. Choose a cantilever, simple supports at both ends, or fixed supports at both ends. Cross-sections can be solid rectangles, round rods, round tubes, or rectangular tubes. It omits attached masses, joint flexibility, prestress, shear deformation, and rotary inertia.

      A cantilever can include a point mass at its free end. The model includes its translational inertia, without rotary inertia, eccentricity, or a gravity-induced axial load. A real attachment with an offset or finite dimensions may require a more detailed model.

      The plate model assumes a thin, uniform rectangular plate with all four edges simply supported. The edges cannot move out of plane but are free to rotate. This differs from a clamped cover or a plate fixed only at a few bolts.

      The ring model assumes a free, thin circular ring with a uniform cross-section. It calculates in-plane ovaling, higher bending modes, or uniform radial breathing. It does not model the axial shape, end constraints, ribs, or attachments of a cylindrical housing.

      Governing equations

      Using the estimate

      A natural frequency describes free vibration. Resonance response also depends on excitation frequency, spatial shape, damping, and attachment stiffness. Use these calculations to screen a design, then check the assembled structure with a suitable model or test.

      Each selected mode is calculated independently. A higher mode result is not the structure's lowest natural frequency. The beam's lowest bending mode in the chosen direction is mode 1; the plate's lowest mode is (1, 1), and the free ring's first elastic bending mode is n = 2.

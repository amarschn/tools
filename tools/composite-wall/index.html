Steady heat flow in series

Every layer carries the same heat flow. A layer with higher resistance takes a larger share of the temperature drop. Increasing the wall area increases total heat flow while U-value and area-normalized R-value stay the same.

This model assumes constant conductivity, uniform area, perfect contact between layers, and one-dimensional steady conduction. It excludes thermal bridges, air leakage, radiation, moisture effects, and heat storage.

Positive heat flow goes from interior to exterior. A negative result indicates heat gain from the exterior. Equal boundary temperatures give zero heat flow.

Default example

A 10 m² wall separates air at 21 °C and −5 °C. Its layers are 200 mm at 0.038 W/(m·K), 20 mm at 0.21 W/(m·K), and 10 mm at 0.72 W/(m·K). Film coefficients are 8 and 25 W/(m²·K).

The result is about 46.95 W, U = 0.1806 W/(m²·K), and US R = 31.44 hr·ft²·°F/BTU. Whole-wall resistance is 0.5537 K/W; multiplying by 10 m² gives 5.5373 m²·K/W.

    Reading an Ashby chart: properties, group shapes, and performance indices

    Compare properties across orders of magnitude

    An Ashby chart places two material properties on logarithmic axes. Pick a chart under Charts, choose a family, or search for a grade. Click any point to tag it, label it, or inspect the observations behind it. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan, and use Fit to return. Click an axis to change its property or scale, or to write a formula such as E^0.5/rho. Formulas use the units shown on the axes.

    What the shapes mean

    Ashby ovals draw one smooth outline around each family, such as Polymers or Metals, or around each material class. An oval is the smallest ellipse around the plotted values. A few isolated materials can sit outside it so one far point does not stretch a whole family, and the panel says how many. Reported interval endpoints are included, and reported bounds are left out. A shape is a drawing aid: it does not estimate a population distribution or the full range of a family.

    Narrow the list

    The selection stack chains filters in order and shows how many materials each stage removes. Limits, regions drawn on the chart, and performance index lines all become stages. Parallel view shows any number of properties side by side and uses the same filters. Dragging along a parallel axis adds a limit to the stack.

    Keep the test conditions in view

    Each point combines observations for one material and compatible conditions. Different temperatures within 20–25 °C can be compared at room temperature; both original temperatures remain visible. Elevated-temperature properties are not paired with room-temperature measurements. Unspecified conditions stay unspecified. Formula axes and parallel views follow the same rule across every property they use.

    Wood Young's modulus is the USDA bending modulus plus 10%, the correction the handbook gives for shear deflection, and it is labeled as computed. The printed bending modulus stays on its own axis. Wood tensile strength was tested green, so it is adjusted to 12% moisture with the handbook's average factor, and only 21 species have it. The rubber sources report tensile strength and elongation, but no small-strain Young's modulus. The chart suggests another property pair when the current axes cannot show a material.

    Rank a design objective

    The Rank tab provides a performance index, its equation, variable definitions, and derivation. Start from a part in the selection stack to pick an index for you. Lines on the plot connect equal index values. These compare source reference values and are not design allowables.

    How the two modes work

    Size a fit solves for interference. The lower drawing limit comes from the pressure needed to carry simultaneous torque and axial load. The upper limit comes from the first shaft or hub stress limit. Check a fit takes an interference band you already have and reports pressure, slip margin, stress margin, and fit loss at temperature and speed.

    1. Required contact pressure

    $$F_t=\frac{2T}{d}$$ $$p_{req}=\frac{SF_s\sqrt{F_t^2+F_a^2}}{\mu\pi dL_{eff}}$$
    • $T$: required torque
    • $d$: fit diameter
    • $F_a$: simultaneous axial force
    • $\mu$: interface friction coefficient
    • $L_{eff}$: credited fit length
    • $SF_s$: slip safety factor

    Torque and axial load draw on one friction budget, so they combine as a resultant rather than being checked one at a time.

    2. Pressure to interference

    $$\delta_{op}=p\,d(C_h+C_s)$$

    The thick-cylinder compliance terms use the shaft and hub diameter ratios, Young's modulus, and Poisson ratio, following the Shigley interference-fit treatment.

    3. Roughness, temperature, and speed

    $$\delta_{eff}=\delta_{spec}-0.8(R_{z,s}+R_{z,h})$$ $$\delta_{op}=\delta_{ref}-d(\alpha_h-\alpha_s)(T-T_{ref})-2(u_{h,i}-u_{s,o})$$

    Surface peaks flatten at assembly, so part of the specified interference never makes pressure. Differential thermal expansion and free centrifugal growth then shift what is left. Rotating stresses are superposed on the Lamé contact stresses wherever a stress limit is solved.

    4. Failure criteria

    Ductile members use von Mises against yield. Brittle members such as gray iron have no yield point and carry roughly three to four times more compression than tension, so they use Coulomb-Mohr against separate tensile and compressive strengths. The material list marks which criterion each preset uses.

    5. Length credit

    Contact pressure and micro-slip concentrate at the ends of the joint. Length past about L/d = 1.5 adds very little capacity, so the tool credits only that much by default. Raise or disable the limit in Expert Mode if your geometry justifies it.

    6. Assembly

    Two questions get answered here. What would let the parts slip together with no press at all, and what does the temperature you actually plan to use leave you to push through. Every route lands on the same axis, the press force it leaves, so they compare directly. Routes nothing can reach are dimmed with the reason.

    $$\Delta d_{req}=\delta_{max}+c_{slip}$$ $$T_{hub}=T_{ref}+\frac{\Delta d_{req}}{\alpha_h d}\qquad T_{shaft}=T_{ref}-\frac{\Delta d_{req}}{\alpha_s d}$$

    For the second question, enter the hub and shaft temperatures you intend. Free thermal growth opens the joint by $d[\alpha_h(T_h-T_{ref})-\alpha_s(T_s-T_{ref})]$, and whatever interference is left sets the press force. That is usually the more useful number: clearing a fit entirely often needs more heat than the part can take, while heating it part way removes most of the force.

    $$\delta_{left}=\delta_{max}-\Delta d_{open}\qquad F_{press}=\mu\,\frac{\delta_{left}}{C_d}\,\pi d L$$

    Both forces are quoted for the tightest interference you would actually build: the allowable maximum when sizing, which is the recommended target plus its tolerance, or the maximum you entered when checking. The panel names the number it used. They share one contact pressure, taken from the interference left after roughness smoothing, and differ only in friction: sliding one part into the other galls, so insertion friction runs above the settled slip value. The multiplier defaults to 1.5 and is adjustable.

    7. Manufacturing window

    The available interference window is graded against ISO 286 IT grades, split evenly between the shaft and the bore. IT6 and IT7 are normal grinding work. Anything tighter than IT5 needs matched machining or select-fit assembly.

    Scope and assumptions

    The model assumes straight axisymmetric cylindrical members, uniform contact pressure, uniform member temperature, elastic isotropic materials, and a common rotational speed. Stresses and compliance are plane stress, which is the standard textbook treatment; a long hub is closer to plane strain, where the joint is roughly ten percent stiffer and the pressure correspondingly higher.

    The upper interference limit is first yield at the most loaded surface. Contained plasticity is permitted by DIN 7190 and is not modeled here, so the tool is conservative for ductile hubs. It does not resolve shoulders, keyways, grooves, taper, fretting life, thermal gradients or transients, temperature-dependent properties, or local end effects. Use FEA and test correlation for high-speed or geometry-sensitive final designs.

What is 3D, and what does this FBD show?

The calculation uses three-dimensional force vectors, application points, and moments. Ground contacts lie in one plane. The FBD projects those vectors into a vertical plane normal to the selected tipping edge; it is a 2D view of the same 3D calculation. G is the combined center of mass, W is weight, I is equivalent inertia, and P1, P2… are applied loads.

The surface-fixed axes are x forward, y left, and z normal to the ground. In the FBD, u points inward from the edge and z points up from the surface. Geometry uses a linear scale; force arrows show direction, with magnitudes listed in the key. Dotted leaders identify application points when arrows are separated for readability.

Ground reactions are required resultants. Individual wheel load sharing and yaw capacity require a separate contact model.

1. The model

Ground contacts enclose a support polygon. The combined forces and moments determine where the ground's normal reaction must act. A point inside every edge has a positive tipping margin. At an edge, the restoring moment reserve is zero.

The model applies an equivalent inertial force opposite to the prescribed acceleration. This is a static or quasi-static equilibrium calculation. It assumes a fixed body configuration and sufficient restraint against rolling.

Suspension, pivoting axles, soft ground, noncoplanar contacts, curb impacts, angular inertia, and motion after tipping require additional models. A four-wheel footprint does not represent the stability triangle of a forklift with a pivoting axle.

3. Reading the results

Positive margin means the required normal reaction lies inside the modeled support polygon. It does not establish a rated operating limit. Sliding, rolling, local tire traction, ground bearing, and structural strength can limit the assembly separately.

The limit search starts with the selected parameter at zero and finds the first edge or contact boundary. Other loads stay fixed. A starting point outside the support polygon is reported explicitly; a later stabilizing load is not treated as a usable margin.

Individual normal forces at four or more contacts generally need a stiffness or load-sharing model. First individual wheel lift, tipping about an edge, and a completed overturn are different events.

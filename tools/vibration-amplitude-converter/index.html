The relationships

For sinusoidal motion at frequency f, with angular frequency ω = 2πf, the three amplitudes are locked together:

$$\omega = 2\pi f \qquad v_{pk} = \omega\, d_{pk} \qquad a_{pk} = \omega^2 d_{pk}$$

Because acceleration scales with ω², the same displacement produces far higher acceleration at high frequency — which is why accelerometers dominate high-frequency measurement and proximity probes dominate low-frequency shaft work.

Amplitude conventions

For a sinusoid the conventions are fixed ratios: RMS = peak / √2 and peak-to-peak = 2 × peak. This tool normalizes your entry to a zero-to-peak amplitude, converts, then re-expresses each quantity in its common field convention (displacement as µm pk-pk, velocity as mm/s RMS, acceleration as g peak).

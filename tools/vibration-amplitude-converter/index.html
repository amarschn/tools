The relationships

For sinusoidal motion at frequency f, with angular frequency ω = 2πf, the three amplitudes are locked together:

$$\omega = 2\pi f \qquad v_{pk} = \omega\, d_{pk} \qquad a_{pk} = \omega^2 d_{pk}$$

Because acceleration scales with ω², the same displacement produces far higher acceleration at high frequency — which is why accelerometers dominate high-frequency measurement and proximity probes dominate low-frequency shaft work.

Peak, RMS, and peak-to-peak

For a sinusoid the conventions are fixed ratios of the zero-to-peak amplitude: RMS = peak / √2 ≈ 0.707 × peak and peak-to-peak = 2 × peak (the full swing, top to bottom). So a value entered as "10 peak-to-peak" describes a smaller signal than "10 peak" — half the amplitude. The results panel shows all three conventions for every quantity so the 2× and √2 relationships are explicit; the convention you entered is highlighted.

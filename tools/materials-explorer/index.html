    Reading an Ashby chart: properties, group shapes, and performance indices

    Compare properties across orders of magnitude

    An Ashby chart places two material properties on logarithmic axes. Select a chart preset, choose a family, or search for a grade. Select any point to inspect the observations behind it. Drag to zoom and use Fit view to return.

    What the shapes mean

    Family view draws one outline per broad family, such as Polymers or Metals. Subgroup view separates material types such as PEEK and PA6, and may split distant clusters. Points only keeps individual observations and range bars. Rounded outlines include reported interval endpoints and a small drawing margin. Reported bounds are excluded from the shapes. The ellipse alternative encloses the same observations; neither method estimates a population distribution or the full range of a family.

    Compare the retained design experiments.

    Keep the test conditions in view

    Each point pairs observations for one material and compatible conditions. Different temperatures within 20–25 °C can be compared at room temperature; both original temperatures remain visible. Elevated-temperature properties are not paired with room-temperature measurements. Unspecified conditions stay unspecified.

    Wood bending modulus is kept separate from tensile Young's modulus. The rubber sources report tensile strength and elongation, but no small-strain Young's modulus. The chart suggests another property pair when the current axes cannot show a material.

    Rank a design objective

    The Rank tab provides a performance index, its equation, variable definitions, and derivation. Lines on the plot connect equal index values. These compare source reference values and are not design allowables.

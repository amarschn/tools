Background & How to Use

What is an Ashby Chart?

Ashby charts (also called material property charts) plot one engineering property against another on logarithmic axes. They were developed by Professor Michael Ashby at Cambridge as a systematic way to compare materials and identify optimal candidates for a given design objective.

Performance Indices

A performance index is a grouping of material properties that captures how well a material performs for a specific structural or thermal objective. On log-log Ashby charts, lines of constant performance index appear as straight isolines. The preferred side depends on the index and axis order. The ranking equation states the objective.

Using this tool

  • Select X and Y axes from the left rail, or click a preset to jump to a classic chart.
  • Choose a performance index to rank materials and overlay isolines.
  • Toggle material families on or off to focus on specific categories.
  • Use Display Options for ranges, envelopes, and labels. Tool Settings controls theme and number formatting.
  • Click any point on the chart to inspect its full sourced property data.

Data

Values come from the Materials database. Each point pairs two observations for one grade and compatible state and conditions. Known temperatures, forms, thickness ranges, and orientations must agree. A grade-level physical property may accompany a named state; its original scope stays visible. Missing properties are omitted. No values are filled from a generic material family.

Intervals appear at their midpoint with optional range bars. Open symbols mark specified bounds, not measurements. Rankings use the shown reference coordinates; a high score at a specified bound does not establish which material has the highest measured performance. The default temperature filter includes 20–25 °C and observations with no stated temperature.

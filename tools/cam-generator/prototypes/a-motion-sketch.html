How the curve is built

Each span between two points is one polynomial in the normalized angle u = (θ − θ₀)/β, where β is the span width. It is solved so that position, velocity and acceleration match at both ends (degree 5). With "Smooth jerk" selected, jerk must also be zero at both ends (degree 7). This is the boundary-condition method from Norton's Cam Design and Manufacturing Handbook.

Stop points force velocity to zero. Pass points take the harmonic mean of the slopes on either side, which keeps the curve from overshooting between monotone points. Set v lets you type the velocity in mm per radian. A constant velocity span is a straight line; its neighbours inherit its velocity and zero acceleration, so the joins are smooth. A span between two stops can instead use a classic law (cycloidal, modified sine and so on).

Acceleration is forced to zero at every point. That keeps the joins smooth but costs some peak acceleration compared with a global spline; the production version could offer both.

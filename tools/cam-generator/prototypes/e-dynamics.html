Spring & Torque

Kinematics says where the follower should be. A spring has to keep it there. When the follower decelerates harder than the spring can pull it back, it leaves the cam (jump) and slams back down. This page finds the speed where that starts, sizes the lightest spring that prevents it, and shows the torque the camshaft motor has to supply.

Assumptions
  • Translating follower, rigid links, single moving mass. Spring surge and follower stiffness are ignored; prototype F shows when that matters.
  • Spring force Fs = F0 + k·s, with s the lift above the base circle. Process load is constant and pushes the follower toward the cam.
  • Torque comes from a power balance, T·ω = Fc·v, which neglects friction and the side force from the pressure angle.
  • Spring sizing picks the rate and preload that keep Fs ≥ SF·(−m·a) at every angle while giving the smallest peak spring force. A small rate floor keeps the answer buildable.
  • Flywheel inertia uses I = ΔE / (Cs·ω²) with speed fluctuation Cs = 0.05.

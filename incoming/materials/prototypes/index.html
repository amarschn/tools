Five compact lookup directions

These pages test information architecture, not branding. They share one search engine and one synthetic stress corpus, so differences in behavior come from the UI rather than the data.

Synthetic fixture. Every name, value, source, and locator is fabricated. Do not use any value for engineering work.
PrototypeCore ideaDecision it tests
01 · Compact index One search field and dense directory rows. Whether a fast typed list is enough without a separate inspector.
02 · Material first Select a material, then find a property inside its record. The strongest no-comparison contract and the simplest ambiguity behavior.
03 · Split inspector Dense results at left, one persistent record at right. Whether repeated lookups are faster when selection never leaves the search context.
04 · Query resolver Resolve category, material, and property intent explicitly. Whether one precise clarification is better than permissive ranking.
05 · Dual-mode lookup Material queries open datasheets; property queries open category-range overviews. Whether one search box can serve both lookup intents without becoming a full comparison workspace.

Queries that expose the decisions

  • plastic strength — both the material and property are underspecified.
  • tensile strength — property mode, grouped by category rather than a flat material list.
  • plastic tensile strength — the same property overview scoped to polymers.
  • engineering plastics — a category, never a data-bearing material.
  • P100 strength — concrete material identity, ambiguous property family.
  • P100 tensile strength — concrete material and precise property.
  • AX60 / AX60 T6 / AX60 T651 — near-identical named states.
  • N6 / N66 — designation collision.
  • glass strength — bulk glass versus fiber-composite category ambiguity.
  • strongest plastic — comparison intent, deliberately unsupported.
  • thermosets — valid empty category.

Working schema hypothesis

Categories are taxonomy only. Concrete materials are independently identifiable subjects. Stable named formulations or processing states sit beneath a material. Conditions such as temperature, direction, moisture, thickness, and test basis stay on observations.

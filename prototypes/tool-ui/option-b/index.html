1 Scope

This sheet estimates tensile stress area, proof load, target preload, and tightening torque for one bolt. The torque equation uses a single nut factor, K, to represent thread and bearing friction.

Use the result to establish a starting point, then qualify the assembly with measured friction or torque-tension testing.

4 Method

Equation (1): tensile stress area
As = π/4 (d − 0.9382p)²
π/4 (10.0 − 0.9382 × 1.5)² = 57.99 mm²

As: tensile stress area; d: nominal diameter; p: thread pitch.

Equation (2): proof load
Fp = SpAs
640 MPa × 57.99 mm² = 37.11 kN

Fp: proof load; Sp: proof strength; As: tensile stress area.

Equation (3): target preload
Fi = αFp
0.75 × 37.11 kN = 27.83 kN

Fi: target preload; α: selected proof-load fraction; Fp: proof load.

Equation (4): tightening torque
T = KdFi
0.15 × 10.0 mm × 27.83 kN = 41.8 N·m

T: tightening torque; K: nut factor; d: nominal diameter; Fi: target preload.

Show intermediate steps
  1. Read d and p from the selected thread.
  2. Calculate As with Equation (1).
  3. Read Sp from the selected property class and calculate Fp.
  4. Apply α, then calculate torque with the supplied K.

5 Assumptions and references

Assumptions

  • Room-temperature steel properties apply.
  • The bolt is loaded axially and remains elastic at the selected target.
  • K represents the complete thread and bearing friction system.
  • The selected thread follows ISO coarse-pitch geometry.

Excluded checks

  • Torque and preload scatter
  • Joint stiffness and external load sharing
  • Embedding, fatigue, stripping, and bearing failure
  • Temperature, corrosion, and relaxation effects
